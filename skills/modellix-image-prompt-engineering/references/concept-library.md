# Concept Library

Use this as a compact starting map, not a list to paste into prompts. Select one
primary anchor because it fits the target medium. Verify factual claims before
using them.

## How to choose an anchor

Evaluate three properties:

1. **Native medium** — what the reference actually designed or is known for.
2. **Structural clarity** — whether its grid, palette, typography, material, or
   composition can transfer to a new subject.
3. **Recognition risk** — whether the model may reproduce a logo, masthead,
   signature, or unrelated text.

If a user asks for a specific reference, preserve their choice unless it conflicts
with their constraints. When no reference fits, describe the visual grammar
instead of forcing a name.

## Product systems and manuals

- **Dieter Rams / Braun** — functional hierarchy, disciplined controls, restrained
  palette, rational product documentation. Strong for devices, packaging, and
  manuals.
- **MUJI** — restrained materials, low-noise product photography, neutral
  typography, unbranded clarity. Strong for household products and packaging.
- **Apple product communication** — singular focal product, precise lighting,
  generous negative space, tightly controlled hierarchy. Exclude logos when the
  subject is not an Apple product.
- **NASA Graphics Standards Manual** — modular identity system, technical
  diagrams, high-contrast wayfinding logic. Strong for technical documentation;
  do not invent mission facts or insignia.

## Editorial and publication systems

- **Swiss International Style** — asymmetric grid, sans-serif hierarchy,
  photography integrated with type, objective information design.
- **Massimo Vignelli** — disciplined grids, limited type families, strong
  hierarchy, systematic identity work.
- **Penguin Books** — recognizable cover systems and series logic. Useful for
  manuals and covers; explicitly prohibit a Penguin logo or copied imprint.
- **The Criterion Collection** — curated edition logic, numbering, archival
  presentation, film-poster sensibility. Use for fictional collection concepts;
  exclude the Criterion name and logo from the image.
- **The New Yorker** — single-scene editorial illustration and literary cover
  framing. High masthead risk; prefer describing the illustration grammar unless
  the user explicitly needs that reference.

## Posters and campaign graphics

- **Bauhaus** — geometric construction, primary forms, functional experimentation,
  and type as composition. Avoid reducing it to random red-yellow-blue shapes.
- **Saul Bass** — reduced cut-paper forms, bold silhouettes, visual metaphor,
  cinematic title-card energy.
- **Constructivist poster systems** — diagonal force, photomontage, limited colors,
  assertive typography. Use carefully outside historical contexts.
- **A24 campaign photography** — contemporary cinematic restraint, tonal
  atmosphere, art-film framing. This is atmosphere-heavy; add composition and
  lighting details.

## Interface and information design

- **IBM Carbon Design System** — enterprise data density, systematic spacing,
  modular components, clear hierarchy.
- **Stripe product interfaces** — polished developer-product communication,
  technical diagrams, soft depth, structured gradients. Exclude brand marks for
  unrelated products.
- **Bloomberg Terminal** — extreme information density and utilitarian hierarchy.
  Useful as a contrast anchor, not as a default for ordinary dashboards.
- **Edward Tufte information design** — high data-ink value, direct labeling,
  evidence-first graphics. Do not invent data to make the graphic look complete.
- **Harry Beck transit map logic** — topological simplification and route clarity.
  Strong for flows and systems; weak for quantitative charts.

## Packaging and material language

- **Japanese pharmacy packaging** — compact information hierarchy, precise labels,
  practical color coding. Describe the grammar rather than inventing a specific
  brand.
- **Mid-century Swiss pharmaceutical packaging** — grid-led labeling, coded
  variants, clinical precision.
- **Brutalist material language** — exposed structure, blunt hierarchy, material
  honesty, deliberate tension. Add the target material and manufacturing context.
- **Luxury editorial packaging** — not a precise anchor by itself. Replace it with
  a real medium, material, print process, and typographic system.

## Illustration and photography grammar

- **Editorial ink drawing** — expressive line, conceptual metaphor, restrained
  palette. Add line weight, paper texture, and composition.
- **Risograph print** — limited spot colors, visible registration offset, grain,
  flat shapes. Strong for posters and editorial illustration.
- **Screen print** — bold separations, hard-edged shapes, tactile ink coverage.
- **Large-format studio product photography** — controlled reflections, precise
  material rendering, simple set, intentional shadow.
- **Documentary environmental portrait** — subject in context, available-light
  realism, meaningful background details.

## References that need extra description

Film directors, broad eras, regional labels, and mood words often transfer only as
surface color. If using references such as Wes Anderson, Wong Kar-wai, "retro,"
"cyberpunk," or "Chinese style," add:

- framing and camera position,
- palette and contrast,
- lighting source,
- production design and material,
- subject behavior,
- what cliché must be avoided.

## Safe substitution pattern

When direct imitation is unwanted or risky, translate the reference into
attributes:

```text
Instead of "[publication] style," use a strict asymmetric grid, one restrained
serif headline, a single conceptual illustration, warm uncoated paper texture,
and no masthead, publication name, logo, or unrelated copy.
```

The goal is not to maximize named references. The goal is to make the visual
decision precise with the fewest assumptions.
