# UGC patterns

Read this when choosing how the product video moves. Offer two patterns that fit, with a one-line reason for each, when the user did not name one. Wait for that choice before writing shot prompts. A pattern the user already named counts as the confirmation.

Use these names. Do not invent a private catalog code.

| Pattern | Use it when | Shape |
| --- | --- | --- |
| interrupt-demo | The first second can stop a scroll with a reaction or a wrong method. | Hook on the face or the problem, then the product, one proof close-up, and a close with the product still in frame. Four shots, about 15 seconds. |
| pain-proof-use | The product replaces one specific annoyance the user named. | Name the annoyance, show it fail, show the product fix it, show it in use, then the close. Fifteen or 30 seconds. |
| unbox-wear | The viewer should see the item revealed and then worn. | Occasion, reveal, one detail, on the body, selfie close. About 20 to 30 seconds. |
| listicle | The user named several peer variants and a reason for each. | One hook, one shot per variant, then a close that holds them together and asks which one. A 15-second cut holds two variants. Four variants need about 30 seconds. |
| maker-story | The user asked for a quieter film of making or placing the object, with voiceover instead of a sales face. | Character and place, hands at work, one detail, the object in the home. Stay near 30 seconds unless the user asks for longer. |

Pick the pattern from the job of the video. A try-on is unbox-wear even if the user also mentioned a caption style. State the phone look in concrete words inside the prompts: window light, a lived-in room, a selfie or a phone on a stand, hands on the product, a cut every few seconds.

## What each beat does

Every pattern is a sequence of these beats. A 15-second video keeps four of them. A 30-second video can use all five, with proof split into two shots.

| Beat | Job | Camera |
| --- | --- | --- |
| hook | Stop the scroll in the first two seconds. The brand name stays out of this line. | Face or problem fills the frame. Eyes to the lens, or one bold failure. |
| reveal | Show what the object is, and one feature. | Product in the hand, label plane toward the lens but with writing left out. |
| proof | Make the claim visible. | Macro, half-face, hand swatch, before-and-after, or the object in use. |
| life | Show where it goes. | Street, bedroom, gym, car, or porch, matching the product. |
| close | Tell the viewer the next action, with the product still visible. | A small gesture: product toward the lens, a thumbs-up, or both items held up. |

## Spoken lines

Write the way a person talks to a friend. One claim per shot. First person. The claim is sensory or situational, and it uses only facts from the user.

These shapes show the rhythm. Fill the braces from the user's facts. Do not paste a sample claim onto a different product.

- interrupt-demo: "Wait — this {attribute} is not what I expected." / "Stop. {Wrong method}. {Why it fails}."
- pain-proof-use: "I hate when {annoyance}." / "Mine used to {failure} in {time the user named}."
- unbox-wear: "I have {occasion} and nothing that works." / "Okay, the {one detail} is the part I wanted."
- listicle: "I don't buy {category} at random. I kept these {count}."
- maker-story: short voiceover over the hands. The last line places the object in the home.

The close is its own line. Use the call to action the user supplied, such as a comment question, "link in bio", or "linking it below". If they want a close and did not give the words, ask once. Do not invent a discount.

## On-screen captions

Captions are a separate list for `modellix-ffmpeg` after download. They are not painted by the video model.

- English captions are shorter than the spoken line. Two to six words. The spoken line can be a full sentence while the caption is the hook, such as "Sticky workout tops?"
- Chinese captions may match the spoken line when that line is already one short sentence.
- Put the words in the lower third, above the bottom rim, so a platform's buttons can cover the edge. One or two lines.

## What this skill leaves out

Phone-filmed UGC keeps skin texture, a slightly messy room, and hands on the product. It does not ask the model to render a brand wordmark, a fake label, a shirt graphic made of letters, or a caption inside the frame. Those words belong in the spoken line or in the caption list. A product still, when the user has one, is the reference for color and shape.
