# Structures

A structure is a way of cutting a commercial. It is not a product category. Apply the chosen structure to whatever object the user named.

Every structure uses the same product lock. A person, when the structure has one, uses the same person lock in every shot they enter. Light, place, and time of day stay consistent unless the structure is `contrast` or `day`.

The last shot is a slow product hold. `contrast` and `day` may end on the person with the product when that image is the point of the film. Say which ending you chose.

## sculpture

The object is the only subject.

- Open on a detail: an edge, a surface, a moving part, or a light on the material.
- Move to a larger view that shows the whole object.
- Show one action the object performs, or one way it is handled, without a speaking person.
- Hold a closer detail that carries the user's claim, if they supplied one.
- End on the whole object, still or slowly moving, in the same light.

## ritual

One person uses the product quietly.

- Open on the place or the hands, before the product is explained.
- Show the person, once, so the lock is visible.
- Show the product entering their hands.
- Show one use. One action.
- End on the product alone, in that same place.

No face speaks to the lens.

## world

The place is designed, and the product belongs in it.

- Open on the place, wide enough to read the palette.
- Bring in the person as part of the place, or stay on the place if no person was confirmed.
- Reveal the product inside that place. Do not cut to a blank studio unless the user asked for one.
- Show one interaction between the person, or the place, and the product.
- End on the product, still in that world's light.

## contrast

A situation changes because the product is used.

- Open on the situation the user named, before the product appears.
- Keep the same person.
- Introduce the product as the turn.
- Show the situation after the change. Same person, same place, different action or feeling.
- End on the person with the product, or on the product alone if the user asked for a packshot.

Do not invent the "before" problem. Use the situation the user described. If they described none, recommend one ordinary situation from the product's use and confirm it before writing prompts.

## day

One person moves through a passage of time. The product returns.

- Use this only for a film of 30 seconds or more.
- Keep one person and one product.
- Time may change. Clothes and the product do not, unless the user asked for a change.
- The product appears in more than one shot, as a prop they actually use.
- End on the person with the product.

Do not add a second story. Cut the day down to the shot count for that length.
