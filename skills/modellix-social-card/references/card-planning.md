# Card planning

Read this when a post needs more than one card, or when the source is a long note, article, or product update.

## Compress before decorating

Start from the source and keep only what the image has to carry:

1. One claim the viewer can repeat after a glance.
2. The promise of the swipe: what they learn by continuing.
3. Four to eight ideas at most. A short note usually becomes four to seven cards. A long article stays inside about eight to ten cards. The rest belongs in the caption.
4. One job per card. A card that makes two arguments should be split or cut.

The image is an outline. Nuance, caveats, and procedure stay in the post body.

## Card roles

Vary the roles. A sequence of identical "title plus three bullets" cards reads as a slide dump.

- Hook: the reason to stop. One concrete object and one consequence.
- Problem: the scene the reader already recognizes.
- Contrast: a misconception beside the actual behavior.
- Checklist: a short list of actions or checks.
- Evidence: a screenshot, product, or number the user supplied.
- Quote: one sentence that can stand alone.
- Close: the three things worth remembering.

## Copy

Write the words that will be painted on the image, then quote them in the prompt.

- Prefer a verb and a consequence over an abstract label.
- Keep a cover to one claim. A second unrelated claim belongs on the next card.
- For a product update, lead with the change the user feels, then the feature name.
- For a 1:1 or a thin 2.35:1 cover, shorten the wide-cover line. Keep the object and the action. Drop clauses.
- Do not invent prices, dates, percentages, or quotes. If the source does not contain a number, the card does not display one.
- On-image language follows the audience. A Xiaohongshu card can be Chinese while this skill's instructions stay English. Keep official product names in their original spelling.

## Visual stance

Pick one stance for the whole set. Name it in ordinary words in the brief.

- Editorial: a considered feature. Serif or songti display, a quiet paper ground, one photograph, small metadata.
- Systematic: an engineered note. One accent color, hairline structure, short labels, numbers the user actually supplied.
- Photographic: the picture leads. Very little type, placed in a quiet area of the photo.
- Poster: one large claim, few words, high contrast, almost no secondary decoration.

Do not mix stances inside one set unless the user asks for a hybrid.

## Prompt shape

Each card prompt describes one finished flat image. Include:

- The aspect ratio, and the pixel size only when it was verified or clearly marked as a working assumption.
- The stance, subject, and background in a few concrete phrases.
- The exact on-image text in quotation marks, with its language, and which line is largest.
- The safe area from `platforms.md` for that placement.
- An instruction to copy the quoted text and not add captions, logos, watermarks, page numbers, or extra claims.

Generate one prompt per card. A shared stance line in the brief keeps the set consistent. Do not ask the image model to render a contact sheet of every card unless the user explicitly wants a single grid image.

## When the user has no picture

Ask once whether they want the card to be type-led, or to include a scene the model invents. Accept their answer and continue. A type-led poster needs no stock photo. If they want a scene, describe that scene in the prompt and keep titles out of the generated texture so the quoted words stay the only text.
